# Scholar's Row — Sitemap & Wireframes

**Working draft v1** — for Justin's review and iteration

---

## Strategic frame

This redesign reflects a deliberate pivot from the original brand guide:

- **Resident voice leads** the homepage and Living Here. Universities and capital partners are deep, serious secondary audiences with their own dedicated pages — but they are not the front door.
- **Tone stays institutional** — measured, exacting, quietly confident. The site speaks vividly about lived experience without slipping into rental-brochure cadence.
- **The audience is the serious graduate student broadly** — PhDs in any discipline, medical residents, law students, visiting faculty. The filter is life stage and intent, not academic field.
- **Pre-operational is acknowledged honestly.** Renderings are labeled. Markets are framed as "where we're building," not "where we operate."

# Page-by-page wireframes

Each page is sketched as a vertical sequence of sections. Section labels in **bold**, content/intent in plain text, design notes in *italics*.

---

## 1. Home

The most opinionated page on the site. Resident-led, but earns institutional credibility before the visitor leaves.

### Hero
- **Headline (display serif):** "What it's like to live where serious work gets done."
- **Subhead (sans):** Scholar's Row is boutique academic housing built for graduate students, medical residents, and visiting faculty — within walking distance of the universities shaping medicine, law, science, and research.
- **Primary CTA:** Explore the experience →
- **Secondary CTA:** For universities & investors →
- *Visual: full-bleed architectural rendering of flagship Santa Clara property, clearly labeled "Concept rendering — Santa Clara, opening 2027." Warm interior light at dusk. Not a stock photo.*

### Section 2 — The problem we solve
- **Eyebrow:** The problem
- **Headline:** Graduate housing hasn't kept up with graduate life.
- **Body (2 short paragraphs):** Universities have grown. Graduate programs have intensified. But the housing stock around them is older, louder, and built for a different kind of student. Serious scholars — medical residents on overnight rotations, PhDs in lab cycles, law students on bar prep — need something the market hasn't built. Scholar's Row builds it.
- *Visual: side-by-side comparison or single quiet hero image.*

### Section 3 — Three pillars (resident-framed)
Three columns or stacked cards. Each is the resident-facing translation of the brand guide's three pillars:

1. **Built for the serious scholar** — Quiet by design, durable by choice, planned around the schedules of people doing demanding intellectual work.
2. **Walking distance from your work** — Each Scholar's Row community sits within a short walk of the labs, libraries, clinics, and lecture halls that define your day.
3. **Operated like a serious place** — Not a leasing office. A platform with one job: making your residence reliable so your work can be the variable.

### Section 4 — A day at Scholar's Row
A narrative band — single paragraph or short numbered sequence. The vivid, lived texture you wanted, but written with restraint.

> 6:14 a.m. Coffee in a kitchen designed for one person to move quickly. 7:08, walking. By 7:23 you're at the lab, the library, the clinic. You come home twelve hours later to a building that is quiet because the walls are quiet, not because nobody is here. You sleep. You do it again.

- *Visual: simple horizontal rule of small interior detail shots — a desk, a kitchen, a hallway, a window at night.*

### Section 5 — Locations strip
- **Headline:** Where we're building.
- 4 cards, one per market: Santa Clara (flagship — distinguished card style), Berkeley, Worcester, Evanston.
- Each card: market name, partner university anchors, "Concept rendering" label if applicable, "Learn more →" link.
- *Visual: each card has its own architectural image — distinct, never the same recycled stock.*

### Section 6 — How we build
- **Eyebrow:** The platform
- **Headline:** A boutique academic housing platform — not a leasing brand.
- **Body:** Scholar's Row is the developer, owner, and operator of every community we build. We choose sites carefully, design for sustained academic life, and stay involved long after opening.
- 3 small icon-led points: *Site selection* / *Design standards* / *Long-term operations*
- **CTA:** How we work →

### Section 7 — Built in partnership
- **Eyebrow:** Partnership
- **Headline:** Built alongside the universities and capital that make this work possible.
- 2 columns: *For universities* | *For investors*
- Each: 2–3 lines of dedicated copy, audience-specific CTA.
- *This is the gateway for the deep institutional content. Don't lead with it. Don't bury it.*

### Section 8 — Insights (3 articles)
- **Eyebrow:** Recent thinking
- 3 article cards. Real articles only — no Lorem.
- "View all →"

### Section 9 — Closing CTA / Get in touch
- **Headline:** Get in touch.
- 3 role-routed cards or buttons:
  - **Future residents** — Join the waitlist
  - **Universities** — Schedule a partnership conversation
  - **Investors** — Request our overview
- *This replaces the single generic "Schedule a Meeting" pattern that's everywhere on the live site.*

## 3. Living Here

The vivid, experience-focused page. The "show me what it's actually like" page.

### Hero
- **Headline:** Designed around the work you came here to do.
- **Subhead:** Scholar's Row communities are built for the realities of advanced academic life — long hours, deep focus, and the need for a residence that holds up.
- *Visual: warm interior at evening — a single desk, a window, a notebook, a lamp.*

### Section — The resident
- **Eyebrow:** Who lives here
- 1 paragraph. Direct, no marketing voice.

> Scholar's Row is built for graduate students, medical residents, doctoral candidates, law students, and visiting faculty. Whether your work is in molecular biology, constitutional law, comparative literature, or surgery, the principle is the same: you need a residence that supports demanding intellectual work and sustained well-being. We build for that.

### Section — Quiet by design
- **Eyebrow:** Designed for focus
- Body: how the buildings are engineered for sound isolation. STC ratings, window assemblies, mechanical isolation. Specific, technical, but accessible.
- *Visual: a labeled cross-section diagram of a wall assembly, or a single quiet interior shot.*

### Section — Walkable to your work
- Body: site selection criteria. Why proximity matters more than amenities. The 10-minute walking radius standard.
- *Visual: an isometric map illustration of a generic Scholar's Row property and its walking radius to academic anchors.*

### Section — Healthy by standard
- Body: indoor air quality, daylight, materials. The brand guide's "human performance" pillar made concrete.
- 4 small specs: *MERV-13 filtration / Low-VOC materials / Daylight in every unit / Operable windows.*

### Section — A day at Scholar's Row (extended)
- The narrative band from the homepage, extended. Five micro-scenes: morning, walk to campus, midday return, evening study, sleep.
- *Visual: a horizontal strip of five small interior shots, time-of-day progression.*

### Section — What's included
- Honest spec list. What every unit has. What every community has. What's not included (e.g., we don't program social events; we don't operate a gym; we don't have a "wellness coach"). The negative space matters.

### Section — How residency works
- 4-step process: Apply / Lease / Move-in / Live.
- Honest acknowledgment that no buildings are open yet; waitlist for first community is in Santa Clara opening 2027.

### Closing CTA
- Join the waitlist (Santa Clara opening 2027) | Tell us about your program

## 4. About

Where the institutional content lives. Universities and investors will read this carefully. Residents may skim it but should leave more confident.

### Hero
- **Eyebrow:** The platform
- **Headline:** A boutique academic housing platform.
- **Subhead:** We develop, own, and operate every community we build. The same team that selects the site is the team that signs the lease ten years later.

### Section — What we do
- 4 short paragraphs:
  1. **Develop** — site acquisition, design, construction
  2. **Own** — long-term holders, not flippers
  3. **Operate** — in-house operations, not outsourced management
  4. **Steward** — measurable performance over decades
- *Visual: simple 4-quadrant diagram.*

### Section — How we choose sites
- 6 criteria, expanded from the live site's Target Markets page. Each gets a short paragraph rather than a one-liner.

### Section — How we build
- **Sustainability** — durable materials, repairability, thoughtful efficiency. Not greenwashed.
- **Technology** — building systems, resident-facing tech (access, maintenance requests, communication). Practical, not gimmicky.
- **Design standards** — the brand guide's "product standard" — acoustics, IAQ, daylight, layout, durability. Made specific.
- *Visual: a small gallery of construction / material details.*

### Section — Insights (thought leadership)
- 6–8 article cards. Real content. Topics from the brand guide: graduate housing thesis, market analyses, site selection, design philosophy.
- "View all" → archive page.

### Section — Press & news
- Where Scholar's Row has been written about. Empty for now — but the slot exists.

### Closing CTA
- Schedule a conversation → routes to Contact's institutional forms.

## 6. Contact

Where the role-based CTAs land. The single most important page on the site for converting visits to conversations.

### Hero
- **Headline:** Let's talk.
- **Subhead:** Three ways to reach us — pick the one that fits.

### Section — Three forms (tabbed or stacked)

**Future resident / Waitlist**
Fields: Name, email, program & university, anticipated start year, notes. Submit → "We'll be in touch as we open communities near you."

**University inquiry**
Fields: Name, role, institution, email, nature of inquiry (housing demand / partnership exploration / other). Submit → "Our team will respond within 2 business days."

**Investor inquiry**
Fields: Name, firm, email, role, nature of inquiry (overview request / specific project / other). Submit → "We'll send our overview materials and propose a meeting time."

### Section — Direct contact
- Email, phone, office address — same as current site.

### Section — FAQ
- 4–6 honest pre-operational FAQs. The current Target Markets FAQ block, expanded:
  - Are you operational today?
  - When will the first building open?
  - How do I join a waitlist?
  - How do universities partner with you?
  - What does the investor process look like?

# Open questions / decisions remaining

These I want your input on before I move to visual design:

1. **Insights content.** Are there real articles ready to publish, or do we design with placeholders and treat content as a separate workstream? The current site has 3 articles that read as placeholder/AI-generated. They should either be rewritten or removed.

2. **Renderings.** Do you have actual architectural renderings for Santa Clara (or any market), or should we design with placeholder labels and source renderings later? This matters a lot for visual credibility.

3. **Press / Logo wall.** Anything real to show, or design the slot empty?

4. **Waitlist mechanics.** Is the future-resident form actually a waitlist that captures and stores leads (Mailchimp / HubSpot / Webflow CMS)? Or just a contact form for now?

5. **Languages.** Brand guide was translated to Brazilian Portuguese. Do we plan for an i18n version of the site, or English-only?

6. **The brand guide pivot — formalize?** This redesign deviates from the guide in deliberate ways (resident-led homepage, broader graduate audience). Worth updating the brand guide itself to reflect the new positioning, so future work is consistent.
